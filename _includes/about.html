02 / How I got here

My path into electronics runs through manufacturing, construction, and CAD. Before my electronic systems degree, I studied architectural construction engineering technology, working with building science, drafting, and BIM.

That background still shapes how I approach hardware: thinking about how something will be assembled, how the pieces fit together, and what someone else will need to understand it.

03 / What I work on

My work spans firmware, board design, instrumentation, and hardware integration. I've written code for ESP32-S3 and STM32 devices, designed and assembled PCBs, and connected embedded nodes to services running on Linux.

Through the Conestoga SMART Research Centre, I've worked on sensing and renewable-energy telemetry. My projects also include distributed acoustic measurement and a mobile robot with custom motor-control hardware.

  • Distributed acoustic measurement ↗Audio acquisition, signal processing, and connected sensor nodes.
  • Wind-energy telemetry ↗Embedded instrumentation and Linux-hosted telemetry.
  • A robot, from board to firmware ↗PCB design, assembly, bring-up, and motor control.

04 / How I approach it

A lot of my work happens between disciplines: tracing a fault from firmware to an interface, bringing up a board, or checking that the data reaching a backend matches what a device is measuring.

This portfolio includes design documents and test reports alongside the projects. They show the implementation decisions, debugging, and validation behind the finished work.
